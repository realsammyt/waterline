# Prototype and design basis

These are experimental gates, not engineering specifications, permit determinations, or a forecast.

| Experiment | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Small optical model | Record a repeated path across shore-equivalent frontal, oblique and side views at roughly 1.05, 1.30 and 1.60 m equivalent eye heights. Capture calm, chop and the silhouette with reflection masked. Add an overhead view and a water-level view: reflections drop out from above, so the nine domes must read as a cluster in plan. Compare before any lighting. |
| Two-dome handoff | Use a mockup to test whether a viewer sees the glow change domes, from two useful angles, and whether the effect still reads at reduced brightness. Compare day, overcast, dusk and dark. Confirm that nothing reads as lit at rest. |
| Shell coupons | Compare translucent silver and honey-tinted finishes in sun, cloud and dusk. Review glare, serviceability, UV durability, compatibility with the water environment, mechanical retention and replaceability before choosing between rigid shell, skin on frame and inflatable. |
| Climbability and fouling | Check that a smooth closed dome offers no handhold or snag from a small boat, and where the required warning signs sit on the work. Leave coupons in water to see how fouling changes translucency, and write the inspection routine from the result. |
| Art-load measurement | Log full-channel, average, standby and startup draw. Test shade and low-charge behaviour. Brightness/runtime must be reduced or decorative light cut before drawing on safety margins. |
| Full floating system | Have the appointed Ontario-licensed marine engineer set site criteria and determine verification for flotation, heel, freeboard, mooring, anchoring and retrieval. Domes present more wind area than ribs; the cluster's wind load and heel need their own review. No generic anchor weight or digital mockup can verify capacity. |
| Nine-dome cost check | Estimate fabrication, electrical, flotation and handling from the current geometry and measured loads. Check against the public call cap after preserving engineering, insurance, marine services, maintenance and contingency. No cost estimate for this form has been validated. |
| Installation and operation | Dry-run lifting, service access and removal; document inspection, weather stops, recovery, crew access, permits and end-of-life reuse before installation. |

The v0.4 diagram's mother disc starts at roughly 9 m across and its largest dome at roughly 4 m across by 2 m high, for optical study only. Actual viewpoint ranges, load, stability, material selection, seasonal water levels and clearances are not surveyed or confirmed. The hardware should disappear visually without hiding its real access and recovery needs.

**Historical power scenario, not a v0.4 estimate.** The earlier art-only model assumed 25 W × 3 h plus 2 W × 24 h = 123 Wh/day, rounded to 150 Wh/day. Its example 450 Wh usable battery represented three idealised days without charging at that rate; 200–250 Wp solar and 600–800 Wh nominal battery were unverified sizing hypotheses for that scenario. For nine domes, measure the cue and standby loads and reassess seasonal shading, losses and reserve before sizing. One additional continuous watt is 24 Wh/day. Required navigation lighting needs independent power and control; it was not included in the historical art load.

Keep mist outside the baseline. An optional calm-conditions clean-water study would need a separate review of power, cleaning, sourcing, servicing, drift, glare and visibility. No atomized harbour water, flame or open-air lasers are proposed in the base work.
