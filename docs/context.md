# Public commission context

WATERLINE is a concept for Toronto’s Harbour Square Park Basin, explored in the context of Waterfront Toronto’s 2027 floating public-art call. The [official RFP](https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/request-proposals-2027-floating-public-art-harbour-square-park-basin), [Addendum 1](https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/addendum-1-request-proposals-2027-floating-public-art-harbour-square-park-basin-0) and [Addendum 2](https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/addendum-2-request-proposals-2027-floating-public-art-harbour-square-park-basin) control, not this repository.

The call set a CAD $100,000 plus HST all-in cap and a December 3, 2026, 5 p.m. Toronto-time proposal deadline. These are call terms, not funding for WATERLINE. The core public encounter is from shore, without boarding or touching. Complementary activities can be considered only from shore or another approved location. The call requires an Ontario-licensed marine engineer and stamped drawings before fabrication; anchoring, navigation, operating, insurance and removal requirements are separate from the creative concept. The statutory, permit, insurance and engineering obligations depend on the actual approved installation.

## What the call asks for

The theme is **OUT OF SCALE: Larger Than Life on the Water**. It invites work that begins with a familiar form, object, story or experience and transforms it through scale and its relationship to water, with water as an active part of the piece. [Six Rings on Mother](rings-on-mother.md) is this project's response.

Other call terms that shape the design: installed by June 4, 2027 and removed in early October; materials must last at least 20 weeks in the water; visible from all sides of the basin, including from boats; low light emission for residents; hard to climb, with warning signs on the work itself; navigation lights required; no site power, solar allowed. Questions to the call close November 26, 2026. The submission is one PDF with up to eight images, sent by email at no more than 25 MB, and AI-assisted material must be identified with the tools used, the role of AI and the artist's own contribution.

The two addenda add that new fabrication must use Ontario-based contractors; that preliminary engineering and fabrication estimates are enough at the proposal stage, with a separate budget line for stamped marine engineering; that the artist's budget carries anchors, the boat and operator, and all installation and removal equipment; that the insurance figures mean at least $5 million of commercial general liability for the artist and $2 million of professional liability for consultants such as the engineer; and that Waterfront Toronto covers its own standard marketing and on-site signage. Anchors go to the lakebed, not the dock wall or pier, in about 4 to 5 m of water over roughly 3 feet of soft sediment. No wind or wave design criteria are prescribed; the artist's engineer sets them after selection.

The project is neither an endorsed installation nor a record of a submission. No claims of any collaboration, approval, field test, license, or award are made here.
