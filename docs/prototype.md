# Prototype and design basis

These are experimental gates, not engineering specifications, permit determinations, or a forecast.

| Experiment | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Small optical model | Record a repeated path across shore-equivalent frontal, oblique and side views at roughly 1.05, 1.30 and 1.60 m equivalent eye heights. Capture calm, chop and the silhouette with reflection masked. Compare the six ribs before any lighting. |
| Two-rib handoff | Use a mockup to test whether a viewer sees light change ribs, from two useful angles, and whether the effect still reads at reduced brightness. Compare day, overcast, dusk and dark. |
| Material coupons | Compare translucent silver finish in sun, cloud and dusk. Review glare, serviceability, UV durability, compatibility with the water environment, mechanical retention and replaceability before material selection. |
| Art-load measurement | Log full-channel, average, standby and startup draw. Test shade and low-charge behaviour. Brightness/runtime must be reduced or decorative light cut before drawing on safety margins. |
| Full floating system | Have the appointed Ontario-licensed marine engineer set site criteria and determine verification for flotation, heel, freeboard, mooring, anchoring and retrieval. No generic anchor weight or digital mockup can verify capacity. |
| Six-rib cost check | Re-estimate fabrication, electrical, flotation and handling from the current geometry and measured loads. Check against the public call cap after preserving engineering, insurance, marine services, maintenance and contingency. No six-rib cost estimate has been validated. |
| Installation and operation | Dry-run lifting, service access and removal; document inspection, weather stops, recovery, crew access, permits and end-of-life reuse before installation. |

The v0.3 diagram’s largest rib starts at roughly 6 m span by 2 m rise above water for optical study only. Actual viewpoint ranges, load, stability, material selection, seasonal water levels and clearances are not surveyed or confirmed. The hardware should disappear visually without hiding its real access and recovery needs.

**Historical power scenario, not a v0.3 estimate.** The earlier art-only model assumed 25 W × 3 h plus 2 W × 24 h = 123 Wh/day, rounded to 150 Wh/day. Its example 450 Wh usable battery represented three idealised days without charging at that rate; 200–250 Wp solar and 600–800 Wh nominal battery were unverified sizing hypotheses for that scenario. For six ribs, measure the cue and standby loads and reassess seasonal shading, losses and reserve before sizing. One additional continuous watt is 24 Wh/day. Required navigation lighting needs independent power and control; it was not included in the historical art load.

Keep mist outside the baseline. An optional calm-conditions clean-water study would need a separate review of power, cleaning, sourcing, servicing, drift, glare and visibility. No atomized harbour water, flame or open-air lasers are proposed in the base work.
