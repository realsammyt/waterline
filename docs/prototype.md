# Prototype and design basis

These are experimental gates, not engineering specifications, permit determinations, or a forecast.

| Experiment | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Small optical model | Record a repeated path across shore-equivalent frontal, oblique and side views at roughly 1.05, 1.30 and 1.60 m equivalent eye heights. Capture calm, chop and the silhouette with reflection masked. Add an overhead view and a water-level view: reflections drop out from above, so the six ribs must read as a weave inside a ring in plan. Measure where the reflection lands at each distance and eye height, and how much of it the ring blocks. Compare before any lighting. |
| Two-rib handoff | Use a mockup to test whether a viewer sees the light change ribs, from two useful angles, and whether the effect still reads at reduced brightness. Compare day, overcast, dusk and dark. Confirm that a held ring is readable at the all-night level and that an unchosen ring reads as dark. |
| Rib coupons | Compare honey-toned anodised and bare silver aluminium tube in sun, cloud and dusk, above water and as a reflection. Review glare, scratch repair, compatibility with the water environment and how the lit segment sits in the tube before confirming the finish. |
| Climbability and fouling | Check that a smooth 15 cm tube offers no foothold from a small boat, that nobody can wedge up where two ribs cross about 0.2 m apart, that the low float's crowned top gives no place to stand, and where the required warning signs sit on the work. Leave coupons in water to see how fouling changes the finish and the float, and write the inspection routine from the result. |
| Art-load measurement | Log full-channel, average, standby and startup draw. Test shade and low-charge behaviour. Brightness/runtime must be reduced or decorative light cut before drawing on safety margins. |
| Full floating system | Have the appointed Ontario-licensed marine engineer set site criteria and determine verification for flotation, heel, freeboard, mooring, anchoring and retrieval. Six slender ribs present little wind area, but each is a 6 m arch on two feet; rib feet, sideways stiffness and heel need their own review. The starting assumption is three lakebed anchors, none to the dock wall or pier, in about 4 to 5 m of water over roughly 0.9 m of soft sediment, with slack for changing water levels. No generic anchor weight or digital mockup can verify capacity. |
| Six-rib cost check | Estimate fabrication, electrical, flotation and handling from the current geometry and measured loads. Check against the public call cap after preserving engineering, insurance, marine services, maintenance and contingency. No cost estimate for this form has been validated. |
| Installation and operation | Dry-run lifting, service access and removal; document inspection, weather stops, recovery, crew access, permits and end-of-life reuse before installation. |

The v0.5 diagram's ring starts at roughly 7 m across and its tallest rib at roughly 6.7 m span by 1.85 m rise, for optical study only. Actual viewpoint ranges, load, stability, material selection, seasonal water levels and clearances are not surveyed or confirmed. The hardware should disappear visually without hiding its real access and recovery needs.

**Historical power scenario, not a v0.5 estimate.** The earlier art-only model assumed 25 W × 3 h plus 2 W × 24 h = 123 Wh/day, rounded to 150 Wh/day. Its example 450 Wh usable battery represented three idealised days without charging at that rate; 200–250 Wp solar and 600–800 Wh nominal battery were unverified sizing hypotheses for that scenario. For six ribs, measure the cue and standby loads and reassess seasonal shading, losses and reserve before sizing. One additional continuous watt is 24 Wh/day. Required navigation lighting needs independent power and control; it was not included in the historical art load.

Keep mist outside the baseline. An optional calm-conditions clean-water study would need a separate review of power, cleaning, sourcing, servicing, drift, glare and visibility. No atomized harbour water, flame or open-air lasers are proposed in the base work.
