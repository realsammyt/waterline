# Foam on Mother · v0.4

**Archived.** Superseded by [Six Rings on Mother, v0.5](rings-on-mother.md), which keeps the mother and the four words and returns to six half rings. Kept as the record of the nine-dome study; its dimensions and estimates do not carry over.

**The foam on a kitchen jar, at the size of a harbour.** Nine fixed translucent domes crowd together on one low amber disc. Calm water finishes the rim domes into bubbles; a wake takes them apart and lets them come back. Nothing moves mechanically, and nothing below the waterline is built.

The disc is the mother: the disc that grows on top of a jar of fermenting tea and is passed from kitchen to kitchen. Here it is the float. The jar is the basin itself, walled on three sides and open to the harbour on the fourth, 112 m by 51 m in the call's description.

## The form

All dimensions are illustrative values for optical study. None is surveyed, engineered or costed.

- **Mother.** One disc about 9 m across, sitting roughly 0.15 m above the water: a buoyant rim with thin layers of open amber mesh stretched inside it. The mesh lies at the waterline. It is not a deck, and there is nowhere flat and dry to stand.
- **Foam.** Nine closed, smooth, honey-tinted domes, about 4.0, 3.2, 2.6, 2.2, 1.8, 1.5, 1.2, 1.0 and 0.8 m across. The largest rises about 2 m. They pack off-centre and share walls where they touch.
- **Out of scale.** A bubble in kitchen foam is a few millimetres across, so the largest dome is about a thousand times life size. A mother in a jar is about 15 cm across, so the disc is only about sixty times. The foam has outgrown what it rests on.
- **Shell.** Starting point: a taut, honey-tinted translucent skin over a light aluminium frame, the way a boat cover is made. Smooth and closed, so it is hard to climb and has nothing to snag. Rigid shells and sealed inflatables stay as fallbacks until the coupons are tested.

## Proposal-stage build basis

First estimates for a proposal, not engineering. The call requires a marine engineer licensed in Ontario to set the real values and stamp drawings before fabrication.

- **Overall size.** About 9 m by 9 m in plan and about 2.15 m above the water at the highest point. Low on purpose: the call asks for simple, light and not too tall.
- **Anticipated weight.** Roughly 1.0 to 1.3 tonnes afloat, most of it the buoyant rim. The nine dome frames and skins together are estimated at under 200 kg. Built in sections that travel by road and lift separately.
- **Materials.** Polyethylene pipe rim, aluminium cross-frame and dome ribs, PVC-coated mesh and tinted film, stainless fixings. No paint, no antifouling coating and no treated wood in the water. Mechanical fixings throughout, so it comes apart.
- **Mooring.** Three lakebed anchors spread around the disc, none to the dock wall or pier, with slack for changing water levels. Three points hold the disc's heading, so the stacked view stays on the same stretch of boardwalk.
- **Light and power.** One low amber source inside each dome, lit one at a time, from a small solar panel and battery on the work. Required navigation lights sit on their own power and control.
- **Warnings.** Do-not-climb notices on the outer face of the rim, readable from a boat approaching from any side.
- **Maintenance.** A boat inspection every two weeks and after any storm: fixings, skins, mooring lines, lights. Skins are patchable in place, and a spare skin for the smallest domes is carried.
- **Installation and removal.** Sections arrive by road, are joined at the water's edge, lowered or assembled afloat, towed into place and moored. Removal runs the same steps in reverse in early October.
- **Afterwards.** The frames and skins are sized to stand again on land as small cold frames or shelters. No recipient is confirmed.

## Four words

The form is built from four working words in the artist's own Honey Foam vocabulary. Each fit is a judgement.

| Word | In a jar | On the basin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mould | The vessel and the conditions we shape | The basin walls, the disc, the dome shells |
| Mold | What grows by itself, spoiling or nourishing | Wake, chop, weather and daylight. Also the literal kind: twenty weeks of fouling on anything that floats. |
| Bubbles | Expression rising, the inside getting out | Each dome with its reflected half, and the glow that rises through them after dark |
| Goo | The shared living culture, passed hand to hand | The mother. Under it, the lake, which a city drinks and nobody can rebuild from parts. After October, the work's next keeper. |

## Walking it

From one stretch of boardwalk the nine stack into a single mound. A few steps along they open into separate bubbles, small ones appearing from behind large ones. From the pier the cluster turns side-on. From the buildings above there is no reflection at all, and the work has to read as cells packed on a disc. From a ferry or a kayak the domes sit at eye level with their reflections under them.

Looking through the translucent domes into the basin is the outside seen in. The glow leaving after dark is the inside getting out.

## Still → Rise → Return

At night one low amber glow passes from the smallest dome to the largest, one dome at a time, then fades to dark. Nothing decorative stays lit at rest. Slow fades, no flashes, no spill toward the residences. Required navigation lighting is separate, independently powered and not part of the score.

The simulator's water presets change the pace and the reflection. They are simulated; no sensor, live data or public input reaches the work.

## Advance only if

1. The nine domes read as foam with the reflection masked, from frontal, oblique, side, overhead and water-level views.
2. A two-dome mockup shows the glow changing domes from at least two viewpoints at low brightness.
3. The work is legible without sound, without mist and without a device in the visitor's hands.
4. The cluster earns a fresh review for wind area, heel, centre of gravity, attachments, handling and climbability, including that the mother offers no walkable deck. Nothing transfers from the rib studies.
5. A written inspection routine says what fouling is cleaned, what is left, and who decides.

## What it must not become

- A literal jar, lid or bottle. The call recommends work that is simple, light and not too tall. The basin is the jar.
- A health or origin story. Kombucha's ancient-origin tales are unattested, and no drink, benefit, recipe, tasting or live culture is part of the work. Nothing is placed in the lake.
- A claim on anyone else's water teachings. The call notes that the lakes are sacred to Indigenous Peoples. The kitchen image is the artist's own and speaks for no one else.
- Something to touch, board or control. It is seen from shore.

## Draft opening for the artist statement

> Look at the top of any jar where something is fermenting and you'll find foam: small domes crowded together, each sitting half in the liquid and finished by its own reflection. Under the foam floats the mother, the disc that grows on a jar of fermenting tea and is passed from kitchen to kitchen. WATERLINE sets both on Harbour Square Park Basin: nine translucent honey-coloured domes, the largest about a thousand times the size of a bubble, on one low amber disc. Only the upper halves are physical. The lake supplies the rest and takes it away again whenever a wake comes through. From one stretch of boardwalk the nine stack into a single mound; a few steps along they open into separate bubbles; from the windows above they are cells on a disc. After dark one low glow rises from the smallest dome to the largest and goes out. The basin is the jar. The water in it belongs to the Great Lakes, a fifth of the surface fresh water on Earth, and like a mother it can be kept and handed on but not rebuilt from parts.

The call caps the statement at two pages and also asks it for dimensions, anticipated weight, materials, maintenance and installation method. The build basis above gives first values for each; none is engineered or quoted.
